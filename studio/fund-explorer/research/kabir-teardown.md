# Teardown — fund and asset discovery on a phone · 22 Sep 2026

**Author:** Kabir (Product Researcher, Fable Design Studio)
**Question:** How do the best Indian retail apps, advisor tools and multi-asset platforms let a person *find* a fund, bond, PMS, AIF, unlisted share or GIFT City product on a 375-wide phone — and what does that say about a chat-led, advisor-facing, multi-asset explorer for Sentinel?
**Why now:** Sentinel's current Fund Explorer (sentence → filter chips → 2–3-fund card → fund page in the row) was rejected: "no recommendation, no navigable journey, shows 2–3 funds and that's it." Three variations are to be planned; this is the evidence they will be argued from.
**Method and limits:** Desk research only — store listings, help centres, product blogs, press, rating methodologies and public web pages of the products, fetched 22 Sep 2026. No Mobbin, no screenshots, no accounts opened. Where a page would not render (Play Store listings, Value Research, Paytm Money's Medium post, Prudent's edge+ page, Wint Wealth's listing page, Morningstar's AWS brochure PDF) I say so and use the next-best source. **Nothing below is filled from memory; every "unverified" is a real gap.** A rendered comparison sheet is the next step — see "What this teardown still owes".

---

## 0. The five-line summary

1. **Nobody in India does discovery as chat.** Every product is a screener, a category tree or a curated list. The only "conversational" claims are Prudent edge+ (goal-planning engine for MFDs) and ET Money Genius (questionnaire → portfolio). That is the gap Sentinel sits in — and the risk: there is no proven pattern to copy.
2. **The best retail explorers give every fund a *peer context* on arrival** — INDmoney ranks a fund inside its SEBI category; Groww's fund page has a "Returns and Rankings" table against category average; Kuvera opens a Comparison tab pre-filled with four category peers. Sentinel's 2–3 cards had no peer context, which is why they read as "numbers thrown at you."
3. **Advisor tools do not have better explorers than retail apps — they have *different jobs* around the explorer:** model portfolios (NJ MARS → "NJ Recommended MF Portfolio", Wealthy Select), a proposal that goes to WhatsApp (Wealthy), client segmentation (Prudent edge+ Business Edge), and brokerage/trail visibility (Wealthy). The fund list itself is usually thinner than Groww's.
4. **Each asset class has its own facet vocabulary and its own card**, and the multi-asset platforms do not blend them: GoldenPi and IndiaBonds filter on rating band / yield band / tenure band / payout frequency; PMS AIF World ranks on 1M–SI returns + AUM + fund manager + a weighted score; Precize filters unlisted shares by *stage* (Pre-DRHP → DRHP Filed → IPO Expected → SEBI Approved). A single "explorer" that reuses mutual-fund facets for bonds will be wrong on day one.
5. **Guided (question → narrowed list) journeys exist but are always separate from the screener** — ET Money Genius, FundsIndia Money Mitr, Kuvera Goals, Grip "Know Your Investor Style", Kristal's advisory algorithm, Prudent edge+ goal planning. No product lets the guided path and the screener share one surface. A chat UI can.

### A.2 Product notes (what is verified, and from where)

**Groww.** Screener facets are "Categories", "Risk", "Ratings", "Fund House" and an "Index Only" toggle, with "Clear All"; 1,764 results; each row shows logo, name, category, 1Y/3Y/5Y, rating and risk ([groww.in/mutual-funds/filter](https://groww.in/mutual-funds/filter)). The fund page runs, in order: header with category tags (Equity · Mid Cap · Very High Risk) → NAV & key details (min investment, fund size, expense ratio, 5-star rating) → **Return Calculator** (SIP projection) → **Holdings (81)** by stock, sector, asset → Minimum investments → "Understand terms" (collapsible) → **Returns and Rankings** (vs category average, rank position) → Exit load → Exit load, stamp duty and tax → **Compare similar funds** (five peers with 1Y, 3Y, AUM) → Fund management (manager bios) → About → Fund house ([HDFC Mid Cap page](https://groww.in/mutual-funds/hdfc-mid-cap-opportunities-fund-direct-growth)). ## F. General patterns (who does each best)

1. **Peer context on arrival, not on request.** INDmoney's rank "within its SEBI category", Groww's "Returns and Rankings" vs category average, Kuvera's Comparison tab pre-filled with four category peers, Morningstar's Due Diligence "vs up to four similar funds". A fund shown alone is a number; a fund shown with its category median is a judgement.
2. **Intent tiles above the facets.** GoldenPi's four purpose tiles ("Highly Rated", "High Returns", "Get Tax Free Return", "Maturing within 3 Years"), IndiaBonds' 12 collections, Paytm Money's investment ideas, Tickertape's named prebuilt screens. The tile is a *pre-filled filter set with a name*, so the first screen is a sentence, not a form.
3. **Verdicts in words.** INDmoney's pros/cons ("does not beat the benchmark consistently"), ET Money's "Fund Verdict", Dezerv's "leaks". A ratio becomes advice when it is spoken.
4. **Asset-native cards.** Bond card = rating + agency, yield, coupon, tenure/maturity, payout frequency, min investment (IndiaBonds, GoldenPi). PMS row = strategy, manager, AUM, returns by period, since-inception, min ₹50 L (PMS AIF World). Unlisted card = indicative price, change, sector, stage (Precize). Nobody reuses the MF card for another asset.
5. **A named, saved shortlist that changes on a cadence.** Tickertape's saved screens with a note ("Top 5 tax-saving ELSS funds"), Wealthy Select's "monthly playbook", NJ's Recommended Portfolio. The shortlist is the durable object, not the query.
6. **Units disclosed next to the number.** PMS AIF World: "1Y returns are absolute; 3Y and 5Y are CAGR." INDmoney: CAGR (lumpsum) / XIRR (SIP) toggle. Groww: "Understand terms" for annualised vs absolute.

## G. Anti-patterns (what makes an explorer feel like numbers thrown at you)

1. **The column dump.** Groww's category table: 13 columns including 1Y/3Y/5Y/7Y/10Y. PMS AIF World: seven return columns. On a 375-wide phone this becomes horizontal scroll or truncation.
2. **A rating with no denominator or agency.** Angel One shows "Rating 0" for unrated funds; Groww's stars carry no agency name on the row. A star without "of what, by whom" is decoration.
3. **Facets as a 50-item checklist.** INDmoney's AMC filter lists ~50 fund houses; Morningstar's Fund Company dropdown 50+. An advisor never thinks "which AMC" first.
4. **A price without meaning.** UnlistedZone: indicative price, no change, no lot, no stage; Altius: no price on the list at all.
5. **Three agencies side by side.** Paytm Money's CRISIL + Morningstar + VR on one fund reads as disagreement, not rigour.
6. **A promise-shaped number.** "9–12 % Fixed Returns" (Wint), "Earn up to 14 %" (Bondbazaar) as the app title, with "not guaranteed" in the footer.
7. **Feature on web, not phone.** Groww compare (at launch), Dezerv and PrimeInvestor overlap, Value Research's selector — the analytical tools live where the advisor is not.
8. **Filters that vanish.** Most screeners show applied filters only inside the filter sheet; Tickertape and Groww ("Clear All") show them as a strip, but none show a *count per facet* on the phone pages fetched.

## I. So what for a multi-asset ADVISOR explorer (Sentinel)

1. **The shortlist card must carry peer context or it will be rejected again.** Every fund/bond/PMS in a Sentinel result needs "rank n of N in <category>" or "vs category median" *on the card* (INDmoney, Groww, Kuvera, Morningstar all do this). Two or three bare cards with 1Y/3Y/5Y is exactly the artefact stakeholders called "numbers thrown at you."
2. **Make the sentence a named, saved object.** Tickertape saves a screen with a note; Wealthy Select is a monthly list. Sentinel's chips should be able to become "Ravi's mid-cap under 0.7 % shortlist — 3 funds — saved 22 Sep", reusable across clients and refreshable. That is the "navigable journey" the stakeholders missed: query → shortlist → proposal, each a thing you can return to.
3. **Vocabulary per asset, chosen by the router, not by the user.** Bonds: rating (with agency), YTM, coupon, tenure/maturity, payout frequency, min investment, secured/unsecured. PMS: strategy, manager, AUM, 1Y/3Y/5Y/SI, min ₹50 L, fee structure. AIF: Cat I/II/III, open/close-ended, lock-in, min ₹1 Cr, tax treatment. Unlisted: indicative price, change, lot, sector, **stage**. GIFT City: currency, inbound/outbound, eligibility (NRI/OCI/resident), min USD 75,000. The DNA file must grow one card contract per asset; PMS Bazaar keeps them in separate sections for a reason.
4. **Facets shown as three bands, not sliders.** GoldenPi and IndiaBonds proved yield / rating / tenure work as 3–4 named bands on a phone ("8–11 %", "AAA (Low Risk)"). Sentinel's chips should default to bands an advisor says aloud ("under 0.7 % TER", "AA and above", "1–3 years").
5. **Intent tiles are how a chat explorer says "what can I do".** GoldenPi's purpose tiles and Paytm's ideas are the pre-chat equivalent of suggested prompts — each is a named filter set. Sentinel's empty state should offer 4–6 such sentences per asset class ("Short-term AAA bonds for parked cash", "Mid caps this client doesn't already hold").
6. **The advisor-only filter nobody ships: "not already held by this client".** Morningstar X-rays the client portfolio; edge+ segments the book; AssetPlus imports CAS — but none puts client holdings *into the fund list as a filter or a badge*. Sentinel reads the book already (`screens/data/book.jsx`); a "Held by R. Sharma · 4.2 %" badge on a result card is cheap and unique.
7. **Overlap belongs in compare, in words, with a threshold.** Dezerv's 0/1–20/20–60/60–80/80+ bands turn a % into a sentence ("Significant overlap — reduced diversification"). Sentinel's `OverlapView` should speak the band, not just the number, and compare should cap at 3–4 (the industry ceiling).
8. **Verdicts as sentences, ratios behind "more".** INDmoney's pros/cons and ET Money's Fund Verdict show that "does not beat the benchmark consistently" is a better first line than Sharpe 0.83. Sentinel already rules "plain English"; the explorer should lead each card with one verdict line and keep Sharpe/Sortino/alpha in an expandable quant row.
9. **Disclose the unit next to the number, every time.** "1Y absolute; 3Y and 5Y CAGR" (PMS AIF World), CAGR/XIRR toggle (INDmoney). F-62 was a unit bug; the explorer must print the unit on the card, not in a footnote.
10. **Compliance copy on the surface, not the footer.** Only Morningstar and PMS AIF World put "past performance…" on the discovery page. SEBI's code wants it in the body, same font, immediately after returns. For an advisor tool this is also protection: every shortlist that becomes a proposal inherits the disclosure.
11. **Eligibility is a filter, not an onboarding wall, for an advisor.** Retail apps gate GIFT City/US at funding (INDmoney, Vested) or at sign-up (Kristal). An advisor serving residents and NRIs needs eligibility as a visible facet ("this client: resident, LRS used 40 %") so ineligible products never appear in the shortlist.
12. **Model portfolios are the advisor's real unit of recommendation.** NJ (largest MFD network) sells a Recommended Portfolio, not a fund list; Wealthy sells a monthly Select; edge+ outputs allocation + schemes. Variation three of the explorer should let a sentence return *an allocation with funds under each sleeve*, not only funds.
